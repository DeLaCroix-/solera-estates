<p><strong>You can sell a property in Barcelona from abroad without travelling to the signing if you are represented by someone with sufficient power of attorney.</strong> To organise the process, it is advisable to deal separately with preparing the property, documentation, commercial decisions and completing the sale.</p>
<p class="seo-company-mention"><a href="https://soleraestates.eu/asesoramiento-venta-inmuebles-barcelona/">At Solera Estates, we help owners living abroad with advice on selling their property in Barcelona, organising the preparation and the decisions that require their approval.</a></p>
<p>This guide is primarily aimed at private individuals looking to sell a home. The aim is to enable you to delegate in-person tasks without losing control of the price, the commitments you make or the money you will receive.</p>
<h2>Key points</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Selling without travelling is possible:</strong> arrange representation and confirm with the notary that the power of attorney is suitable.</li>
<li><strong>Living abroad does not in itself determine your tax residence:</strong> check it before calculating taxes and withholdings.</li>
<li><strong>Review the property before committing to dates:</strong> ownership, charges, occupancy and technical documentation may affect the transaction.</li>
<li><strong>If you sell as a non-resident without a permanent establishment,</strong> the buyer must generally withhold 3% on account of your tax.</li>
<li><strong>There are obligations after signing:</strong> Form 211 has a one-month deadline; Form 210 for the transfer must be filed within three months after that first month has elapsed.</li>
<li><strong>The first step is to organise the file:</strong> define who will coordinate the arrangements and which decisions require your approval.</li>
</ul>
<h2>What to decide before putting the property on the market</h2>
<h3>Who coordinates the process and what you are authorising</h3>
<p>Separate estate agency support from representation for signing. Coordinating viewings, requesting quotes or gathering documents does not mean having the authority to accept contractual commitments on your behalf.</p>
<p>Set out in writing the scope of the engagement, fees, expenses requiring authorisation and how offers will be communicated. If several professionals are involved, agree who will centralise information and who will handle each procedure.</p>
<h3>The property’s actual situation</h3>
<p>Start by identifying all owners and checking whether there are mortgages, other rights or circumstances that need to be resolved. If there is an inheritance, usufruct, tenancy or protected status, request a review before promising vacant possession or a property free of charges.</p>
<p>When several owners live in different countries, it is advisable to agree from the outset on an indicative price, acceptable terms and how each person will take part.</p>
<h2>How to arrange a power of attorney to sell without travelling to Barcelona</h2>
<p>The power of attorney must allow the representative to carry out the specific actions you are delegating. <strong>Authorisation to show the property is not a substitute for sufficient power of attorney to complete its sale.</strong></p>
<h3>Define the powers and limits</h3>
<p>Before granting it, review with the notary what it needs to include. You may set limits regarding the property, price or terms, and decide whether the attorney-in-fact may:</p>
<ul>
<li>Sign preliminary documents and the deed of sale.</li>
<li>Handle arrangements relating to the mortgage.</li>
<li>Receive payments or give instructions regarding payment.</li>
<li>Complete tax procedures connected with the transaction.</li>
</ul>
<p>Do not include broad powers simply for convenience if you do not need them. It is preferable to agree on the content before signing the document in another country.</p>
<h3>Check the options available in your country</h3>
<p>You can enquire about granting the power before a Spanish consulate with notarial functions or before a notary in the country where you live. In the latter case, confirm any apostille or legalisation formalities and official translation that may be required.</p>
<p>Send the draft in advance to the notary who will be involved in Barcelona. This will allow them to assess its suitability and the documentation required to use it in the transaction.</p>
<h3>Do not confuse a scanned copy with the documentation required for signing</h3>
<p>A scanned file may be useful for an initial review, but it does not replace the official copy of the power of attorney. Confirm with the notary how this copy must be received and avoid leaving its availability until the day of the deed.</p>

<h3>What to do if documentation is missing</h3>
<p>First identify what is missing, who can obtain it and whether a visit to the property is required. Do not set a signing date solely because a document has been requested.</p>
<p>The Government of Catalonia sets out the habitability and energy efficiency documentation that must accompany the transfer. The ITE documentation and certificate of fitness also require review according to the building’s situation.</p>
<h2>How to prepare the property and manage marketing from abroad</h2>
<h3>Organise keys, upkeep and viewings</h3>
<p>Appoint someone responsible for the keys and establish how access will be authorised. It is also advisable to agree who will check the condition of the property, deal with issues and coordinate visits from technicians or prospective buyers.</p>
<p>If it is vacant, request an initial inspection with up-to-date photographs. Before commissioning works, compare their cost, the time required and their usefulness for the sale. A major renovation should not be assumed to be necessary or recoverable through the price.</p>
<h3>Set the price using information and compare complete offers</h3>
<p>Ask for the proposed price to explain the comparable properties used, their differences and whether they are asking prices or completed transactions. Agree when to review the strategy if the market response does not match expectations.</p>
<p>To assess an offer, also request information about financing, the timetable, terms and items included in the sale. Do not decide based on the figure alone: compare the full set of commitments you would be taking on.</p>
<h3>Maintain a simple tracking system</h3>
<p>Use a single record for offers, documents and decisions. Define an update frequency and a channel for urgent matters. Time differences should not force you to approve a price reduction or contract without having time to review it.</p>

<h3>Check the effect of the document</h3>
<p>Not every payment allows you to withdraw from the sale by returning the amount received. Where the Catalan Civil Code applies, withdrawal deposits must be expressly agreed. The document should also be reviewed in relation to the buyer’s financing and the consequences should it be refused.</p>
<h3>Align dates with outstanding arrangements</h3>
<p>Before committing to the date of the deed, confirm representation, technical documentation and mortgage arrangements. Clearly set out the handover terms, included furniture, charges to be cancelled and the treatment of outstanding expenses.</p>
<p>If an issue remains unresolved, ask for it to be properly reflected in the contract rather than relying on a verbal agreement.</p>
<h2>Taxes and costs: how much money will you actually receive?</h2>
<h3>Living abroad does not automatically mean you are a non-tax resident</h3>
<p>Tax residence depends on personal circumstances and legal criteria, not only on the address where you live or your nationality. Before calculating the sale, confirm the applicable regime and how to prove it.</p>
<p>If there are several owners in different situations, analyse each person’s share. Also check whether there are obligations in the country of residence and how the double taxation treaty may apply.</p>
<h3>Capital gains tax and the 3% withholding are different concepts</h3>
<p>For a non-resident individual selling without a permanent establishment, the general rate applicable to property gains is <strong>19%</strong>. It does not apply to the full price: the taxable gain is calculated taking into account the acquisition and transfer values, as well as allowable expenses and adjustments.</p>
<p>In turn, the buyer must generally withhold <strong>3% of the consideration corresponding to the non-resident seller</strong> and pay it using Form 211. This is an advance payment of the seller’s tax, not an additional tax or necessarily their final tax cost.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong><a href="https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/procedimientoini/GF01.shtml">Form 211</a>:</strong> the buyer pays the withholding within one month of the transfer and provides the seller with the relevant copy.</li>
<li><strong><a href="https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/procedimientoini/GF00.shtml">Form 210</a>:</strong> the seller declares the transfer within the three months following the end of that first month, regardless of the result of the self-assessment.</li>
</ul>
<p>If the withholding exceeds the resulting tax liability, the excess may be claimed back through the relevant return.</p>
<h3>Municipal capital gains tax is handled separately</h3>
<p>Municipal capital gains tax is not the same as tax on capital gains. In Barcelona, the general deadline to self-assess a sale and pay, where applicable, is <strong>30 working days</strong>.</p>
<p>Where the seller is a non-resident individual in Spain, the buyer acts as a substitute taxpayer before the City Council. It is advisable to have this procedure resolved before signing.</p>
<p>If there has been no increase in the value of the land, non-liability may apply, but it must be evidenced and the transfer declared: it is not enough simply to omit the procedure.</p>
<h3>Distinguish between funds available at signing and the final outcome</h3>
<p>Request an estimate that separates the price, mortgage balance, agreed fees, documentation, powers of attorney, translations and applicable taxes. Also distinguish between amounts already received and those still to be collected.</p>
<p>Do not add the 3% withholding and the full non-resident income tax liability as though they were two independent taxes. The funds available at signing are different from the financial outcome after taxes and costs have been settled.</p>
<h2>How to organise signing and payment from abroad</h2>
<h3>Confirm banking arrangements before setting the date</h3>
<p>If you wish to receive the money in a foreign account, agree the payment method in advance with the parties, the notary and the entities involved. Confirm account ownership, banking instructions and the documentation worth preparing to justify the origin of the funds.</p>
<p>Check transfer and currency exchange costs before deciding. As a security measure, verify any change to the recipient account through a channel other than the message communicating the change.</p>
<h3>Review the document and transaction settlement</h3>
<p>Ask to review the draft deed and breakdown of payments before signing. Check that they match what was agreed regarding the price, advance payments, withholdings, mortgage and handover of the property.</p>
<p>The notary will verify the means of payment and record them in the deed. Coordinate handover of the keys with completion and the agreed payment arrangements.</p>
<h2>What needs to be organised after selling</h2>
<p>The transaction does not end with signing. Assign responsibility for tax procedures, obtaining supporting documents and the necessary notifications to the owners’ association, utility providers and insurer.</p>
<p>Keep the deed, proof of payment, invoices and tax returns. If there was a mortgage, also check its removal from the Land Registry: repaying the loan does not mean the charge has disappeared from the Registry.</p>
<p>Before closing the file, request a list of completed procedures, outstanding amounts and documents you will receive. This organisation allows you to follow the transaction from another country without depending on scattered communications.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions about selling a property in Barcelona from abroad</h2>
<details class="seo-faq-item">
<summary><h3>Can I sign everything by videoconference from abroad?</h3></summary>
<p>This should not be assumed. Certain powers of attorney for specific acts may be granted by videoconference before a Spanish notary, subject to verification of the requirements. This possibility does not automatically make the deed of sale for a home an entirely online procedure. Ask the notary which route your transaction allows.</p>
</details>
<details class="seo-faq-item">
<summary><h3>Is a power of attorney I granted years ago valid?</h3></summary>
<p>It may be valid if it remains in force and contains sufficient powers for the transaction. The notary must review its content and the documentation proving it. It is not enough for the document to be called a general power of attorney or to send a photograph.</p>
</details>
<details class="seo-faq-item">
<summary><h3>Does being Spanish avoid the 3% withholding when selling?</h3></summary>
<p>No. Spanish nationality does not by itself exclude the withholding. What matters is the seller’s tax regime and tax residence. A Spanish owner may be taxed as a non-resident, while a foreign owner may be tax resident in Spain.</p>
</details>
<details class="seo-faq-item">
<summary><h3>Does the 3% withholding apply even if I sell at a loss?</h3></summary>
<p>Generally, yes, where the sale is subject to the non-resident regime without a permanent establishment. The withholding is calculated on the consideration, not on the gain. If it exceeds the resulting tax, you can claim the excess through Form 210; the refund is not issued automatically on signing.</p>
</details>
<details class="seo-faq-item">
<summary><h3>Can I sell remotely a home that still has a mortgage?</h3></summary>
<p>Yes, but you must coordinate the outstanding balance and how the financing will be cancelled or resolved in the transaction. Prepare the documentation with the bank and notary. Distinguish between repaying the loan and removing the charge from the Land Registry.</p>
</details>
<details class="seo-faq-item">
<summary><h3>How long does it take to organise a sale from abroad?</h3></summary>
<p>There is no single timeframe. It depends on the available documentation, representation, the property’s situation and the buyer’s financing. To set a realistic date, first identify the outstanding arrangements and confirm their timings with those who need to carry them out, rather than committing to a signing date in advance.</p>
</details>
