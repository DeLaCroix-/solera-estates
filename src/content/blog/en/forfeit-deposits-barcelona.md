<p>Signing a deposit contract is one of the most important stages in buying a home. This document sets out the price, deadlines and, above all, what happens if the buyer or seller ultimately does not proceed with the transaction.</p>
<p>In Barcelona, it is particularly important to distinguish between <strong>confirmatory deposits and forfeit deposits</strong>. They do not have the same consequences.</p>

<h2>Summary: what you need to know before signing a deposit contract in Barcelona</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>In Catalonia, unless otherwise stated, money paid is deemed to be a confirmatory deposit.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Forfeit deposits must be expressly agreed</strong>.</li>
<li>If the buyer withdraws under a forfeit deposit agreement, they will normally lose the amount paid.</li>
<li>If the seller withdraws, they must return the deposit <strong>in double</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>The law does not require a 10% deposit.</strong> The amount is negotiated between the parties.</li>
<li>If the purchase depends on a mortgage, Catalan law provides specific protection where the contract includes this financing.</li>
<li>A mortgage refusal <strong>should not be treated as a simple generic clause</strong>: the way it must be evidenced and the relevant deadline need to be properly drafted.</li>
<li>Before paying a significant amount, we recommend checking ownership, charges, occupants, the property's documentation and the terms of the sale and purchase.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What are forfeit deposits?</h2>
<p><strong>Forfeit deposits</strong> are a sum of money paid by the buyer under a sale and purchase agreement, allowing either party to withdraw from the transaction while accepting a previously agreed financial consequence.</p>

<p>If the buyer withdraws, they lose the deposit.</p>
<p>If the seller withdraws, they must return it in double.</p>
<p>For example, if a property is sold for 500,000 euros and the parties agree a forfeit deposit of 50,000 euros:</p>
<ul>
<li>If the buyer withdraws, they may lose the 50,000 &euro;.</li>
<li>If the seller withdraws, they must return 100,000 &euro;.</li>

<p>However, this consequence should only be assumed where <strong>forfeit deposits</strong> have genuinely been agreed.</p>
<h2>Deposits in Catalonia: Article 621-8 of the Catalan Civil Code</h2>
<p>For a property sale and purchase in Barcelona, we must pay particular attention to the <a href="https://www.boe.es/biblioteca_juridica/codigos/codigo.php?id=150&amp;modo=2&amp;nota=0&amp;tab=2"><strong>Catalan Civil Code</strong>.</a></p>
<p>Its <a href="https://www.iberley.es/legislacion/articulo-6218-codigo-civil-cataluna">Article 621-8 </a>establishes a fundamental rule:</p>
<p><strong>money paid by the buyer to the seller is, by default, deemed to be paid as a confirmatory deposit.</strong></p>
<p>For it to be considered a forfeit deposit, this must be expressly agreed.</p>
<p>This means we do not recommend using only ambiguous wording such as:</p>
<p>&ldquo;the buyer pays 30,000 euros as a deposit&rdquo;.</p>
<p>If the parties wish to be able to withdraw by forfeiting the deposit or returning it in double, this must be clearly stated in the contract.</p>
<h2>And what does Article 1454 of the Civil Code say?</h2>
<p>The<a href="https://www.iberley.es/legislacion/articulo-1454-codigo-civil"> Article 1454 of the Spanish Civil Code </a>sets out the traditional rule under which the buyer may withdraw by forfeiting the deposit and the seller may do so by returning it in double.</p>
<p>However, when analysing a property transaction in Catalonia, we must take into account the specific Catalan regulations, which also address matters that are particularly important when buying a home.</p>
<h2>Forfeit deposits vs. confirmatory deposits</h2>
<p>The difference is very important.</p>
<h3>Forfeit deposits</h3>
<p>They allow the parties to withdraw from the contract while accepting the agreed cost.</p>
<p>The buyer forfeits the deposit and the seller returns double if they are the one who withdraws.</p>
<h3>Confirmatory deposits</h3>
<p>They serve as confirmation of the sale and purchase and normally as an advance payment towards the price.</p>
<p>They do not, in themselves, grant a free right to withdraw from the transaction.</p>
<p>In the event of breach, claims to enforce or terminate the contract and, where applicable, damages may come into play.</p>
<p>This is why a single word in a contract can completely change the legal position of the buyer and seller.</p>
<h2>How much is normally paid as a deposit?</h2>
<p><strong>There is no legally mandatory percentage.</strong></p>
<p>In many residential transactions, around 10% of the price is used as a reference, but the amount can be freely negotiated.</p>
<p>For a property worth 700,000 euros, for example, paying 10% means committing 70,000 euros.</p>
<p>That is why we recommend not treating a deposit contract as a simple &ldquo;reservation&rdquo;.</p>
<p>It is a contract with significant financial consequences.</p>
<h2>What happens if the bank does not approve the mortgage?</h2>
<p>This is one of the most important points for any buyer in Barcelona.</p>
<p>The answer should not be reduced to:</p>
<p><strong>&ldquo;if the bank does not approve the mortgage, you lose the deposit&rdquo;.</strong></p>
<p>The Catalan Civil Code contains specific provisions on this matter.</p>
<p>Article 621-49 provides that, <strong>if the contract states that all or part of the price will be financed through a credit institution</strong>, the buyer may withdraw when they provide documentary evidence, within the agreed period, that the designated institution has refused the financing.</p>
<p>This option applies unless otherwise agreed and provided that the bank's refusal is not due to the buyer's own negligence.</p>
<p>In that case, the seller must return the sums received and, where applicable, the forfeit deposit.</p>
<h3>What does this mean when drafting the deposit contract?</h3>
<p>If you need a mortgage, we recommend that the contract clearly explains:</p>
<ul>
<li>That the purchase depends on bank financing.</li>
<li>The approximate amount to be financed.</li>
<li>The deadline for applying and receiving a response.</li>
<li>How the refusal must be evidenced.</li>
<li>What happens to the deposit if the financing is refused.</li>
</ul>
<p>The clearer the contract, the less room there will be for subsequent dispute.</p>
<h2>Example: I am buying a flat in Barcelona and the bank refuses the mortgage</h2>
<p>Let us imagine a property worth 600,000 euros.</p>
<p>The buyer pays 60,000 euros as a deposit and needs a mortgage of 350,000 euros.</p>
<p>If the contract records the intended financing and the institution subsequently refuses the loan, the situation must be analysed under Catalan law and according to the specific wording of the contract.</p>
<p>This is very different from a buyer who signs without mentioning any financing and later decides they do not want to use their own funds.</p>
<p>The documentation and the contract matter.</p>
<h2>Can the seller withdraw because they receive a better offer?</h2>
<p>If forfeit deposits have been properly agreed, the seller also has the option of withdrawing.</p>

<h2>Can the buyer change their mind simply because they have found another property?</h2>
<p>They can withdraw if the contract establishes genuine forfeit deposits.</p>
<p>However, as a general rule, they must accept the loss of the deposit paid.</p>
<p>Finding another property, changing their mind or deciding that the price is no longer attractive is not equivalent to a protected refusal of financing or a breach by the seller.</p>
<h2>Can the deposit be recovered?</h2>
<p>It depends on why the sale and purchase is not completed and on what the contract says.</p>
<p>Situations that need to be analysed include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Financing being refused under the conditions provided for by law and the contract.</li>
<li>Breach by the seller.</li>
<li>Ownership issues.</li>
<li>Unexpected charges.</li>
<li>Planning or documentary circumstances incompatible with what was agreed.</li>
<li>Failure to fulfil a condition expressly set out in the contract.</li>
</ul>
<p>We do not recommend automatically assuming that any issue allows the deposit to be recovered.</p>
<p>First, the specific cause and the signed wording must be reviewed.</p>
<h2>What to check before signing a deposit contract in Barcelona</h2>
<p>Before paying tens of thousands of euros, we recommend checking, at a minimum, the legal and documentary status of the property.</p>
<h3>Ownership</h3>
<p>We must check who the true owner is and who has the authority to sell.</p>
<h3>Charges</h3>
<p>Mortgages, attachments or other charges must be known before signing, and it must be clear how they will be cancelled.</p>
<h3>Occupation and tenancies</h3>
<p>It is essential to know whether the property is vacant, rented, occupied or subject to any third-party rights.</p>
<h3>Price and payment terms</h3>
<p>The total price, the amounts already paid and how the balance will be paid should be specified.</p>
<h3>Completion date</h3>
<p>The contract should set a specific date or an unambiguous mechanism for determining the deadline for attending before the notary.</p>
<h3>Financing</h3>
<p>If you need a mortgage, this point must be expressly addressed.</p>
<h3>Consequences of withdrawal</h3>
<p>It must be clear that forfeit deposits are agreed and what happens if either party withdraws.</p>
<h2>An additional protection available under Catalan law</h2>
<p>The Catalan Civil Code also provides for an option that is particularly interesting in certain property transactions.</p>
<p>When forfeit deposits are agreed for a maximum period of <strong>six months</strong> and are deposited with a notary, they can be recorded in the <strong>Property Registry</strong>.</p>
<p>In that case, the property is subject to repayment of the deposit on the terms provided by law.</p>
<p>This is not the usual structure for every transaction, but it may be relevant where significant amounts are involved or circumstances justify additional protection.</p>
<h2>International buyers and deposits in Barcelona</h2>
<p>A significant proportion of the property transactions we support at <strong>Solera Estates</strong> involve international buyers.</p>
<p>In these cases, we recommend anticipating matters that may delay the transaction:</p>
<ul>
<li>Obtaining an NIE.</li>
<li>A bank account and international transfers.</li>
<li>Proof of source of funds.</li>
<li>Financing in Spain.</li>
<li>Powers of attorney if the buyer cannot travel.</li>
<li>Banking timescales.</li>
</ul>
<p>These elements should be coordinated with the deadline set out in the deposit contract.</p>
<p>There is no point accepting a very short completion deadline if we know from the outset that a significant part of the documentation or funds will come from abroad.</p>
<h2>Conclusion: do not sign a deposit contract as if it were a simple reservation</h2>
<p><strong>Forfeit deposits in Barcelona</strong> can be a very useful tool for giving both buyer and seller certainty while the completion deed is being prepared.</p>
<p>However, they can involve tens of thousands of euros.</p>
<p>Before signing, we must be clear about:</p>
<p><strong>what type of deposit it is + how much is being paid + when completion takes place + what happens if the mortgage falls through + what happens if either party withdraws.</strong></p>
<p>In Catalonia, moreover, the specific provisions of the Catalan Civil Code establish rules that must be taken into account, particularly regarding buyer financing.</p>
